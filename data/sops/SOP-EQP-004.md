## Purpose

This procedure describes how the Water-for-Injection (WFI) generation and distribution system should be operated, sanitized, and sampled so that water of a suitable quality is supplied to the point-of-use outlets, and it should be understood that the still, the storage tank, and the hot recirculating distribution loop are all part of a single system that should be maintained in a state of control at all times so that water of appropriate chemical and microbial quality is available as required for compounding, rinsing, and other GMP uses across the site.

## Scope

This procedure applies to routine operation of the WFI still (EQP-WFI-01), the WFI storage tank, and the distribution loop and its point-of-use outlets. It applies to qualified utility operators and to the engineering technicians who provide support as necessary.

## Responsibilities

Utility operators should operate and monitor the system as written, engineering technicians should provide maintenance and troubleshooting support in a timely manner, and QC should perform sampling and testing as required so that the water quality is verified periodically against the applicable limits.

## Equipment and Materials

- Multiple-effect WFI still EQP-WFI-01 and heat-exchange skid
- Jacketed WFI storage tank with vent filter and spray ball
- Hot water-recirculating distribution loop with point-of-use valves
- Online conductivity and TOC instrumentation

## Procedure

Step 1: At the start of each shift the operator should confirm that the storage tank temperature is being maintained at or above the hot-loop setpoint that keeps the water in a self-sanitizing condition, and the operator should verify that the loop return flow and the online conductivity and TOC readings are within their normal operating ranges, because the system should not be used to supply product-contact water whenever any of these parameters appears to be trending outside of what is appropriate.

Step 2: The distribution loop should be maintained in continuous recirculation so that no dead-leg or stagnant condition is allowed to develop, and if a point-of-use outlet has not been used for a period that is considered sufficient to warrant it, that outlet should be flushed for an adequate time before water is drawn for use.

Step 3: Water should be drawn only from qualified point-of-use outlets and, before each use, the outlet should be flushed as necessary and the local conductivity should be confirmed to be suitable, and the operator should record the outlet identification, the flush, and the reading in the use log in accordance with SOP-DOC-001.

Step 4: Periodic sanitization of the still, tank, and loop should be performed in accordance with the sanitization schedule, and the sanitization of any product-contact vessel that is served by the system should be coordinated with the clean-in-place activities that are described in SOP-CLN-002 so that the overall cleaning state of the connected equipment is maintained as required.

Step 5: Routine and for-cause sampling should be coordinated with QC so that conductivity, TOC, and microbial and endotoxin attributes are tested against the compendial limits, and this testing should be performed in accordance with SOP-QC-004; whenever a result approaches or exceeds a limit, the system should be placed on hold and an investigation should be initiated in a timely manner.

Step 6: At the end of the operating period the operator should confirm that the system has been returned to its normal recirculating state, and the logbook entries should be reviewed for completeness and accuracy before the shift is handed over.

## Acceptance Criteria

Water should be considered suitable for use only when the online conductivity and TOC are within the compendial limits, the loop temperature is maintained in the self-sanitizing range, and the most recent microbial and endotoxin results are acceptable. Instrumentation used to make these judgments should be qualified and maintained in accordance with USP <1058>.
