## 1. Purpose

This procedure defines how to monitor compressed gases and utility points in Building 4.
It exists so that each gas that contacts product or product-contact surfaces stays within
its quality limits.

## 2. Scope

This procedure applies to compressed air, nitrogen, and other process gases at each
point of use. It covers routine sampling, testing, and trending of these utilities.

## 3. Responsibilities

EM analysts must sample each point on schedule. Engineering must maintain the filters
and regulators. The EM Program Lead must review the trend data. Quality Assurance must
approve the acceptance limits.

## 4. Monitoring Points and Frequency

1. Sample each product-contact gas point at least monthly.
2. Sample non-contact utility points at least quarterly.
3. Sample any point after a filter change or a repair.
4. Record each point, date, and result on the utility log.

## 5. Test Parameters

1. Measure the total viable count in the gas stream.
2. Measure non-viable particles at 0.5 µm and 5.0 µm.
3. Measure water content as dew point for compressed air and nitrogen.
4. Measure oil content where the gas contacts product.
5. Confirm delivery pressure at the point of use.

## 6. Acceptance Criteria

1. Compare each result against the approved utility limits table.
2. Accept the point only when every parameter is within limit.
3. Flag any out-of-limit result to Quality Assurance the same day.
4. Place the affected point on hold until the cause is resolved.

## 7. Documentation

1. Enter each result into the utility monitoring record.
2. Trend the results for each point monthly.
3. Report adverse trends to the EM Program Lead.
4. Retain all records per the site retention schedule.
